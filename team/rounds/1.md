## Findings

1. **Blocking. Home page (/) and /index.md: LinkedIn is cited as a source that was read.**
   What is wrong: the line "He says LinkedIn is the fastest route to reach him." cites two sources. The second is `linkedin.com/in/diniscruz`, with the tooltip "Source: linkedin.com/in/diniscruz, read 19 November 2026" (src/index.html line 55). The research notes and /sources/ both say LinkedIn was blocked (HTTP 429 and 999) and was not read. index.md repeats the same source link, so an AI reader would take LinkedIn as evidence for the claim. This is a false statement about where a fact came from, on a site that tells visitors "Every fact on this site links to where we read it".
   Fix: remove the LinkedIn link from the `srcs` span on the home page and in src/index.md, and leave diniscruz.ai/about as the only source. If you want a link to the profile there, add it as a plain link outside the source marker ("reach him on LinkedIn").

2. **Blocking. /cv/ "His deck" (and index.md): a claim about the deck that rests only on a search snippet.**
   What is wrong: "Open it and look through it before you read on. It is the best part of this page, and it is short." The team has not seen the deck. "It is short" can only come from the snippet slide count (12), and the next paragraph says the page does not repeat the number of slides. "The best part of this page" is a judgement about content nobody on the team has read.
   Fix: replace it with something true, for example "Open it and look through it before you read on. We could not open it ourselves, so this page does not describe its slides."

3. **Minor. Home (/) lead cards, and /llms.txt.** The recommendations card says "Dinis's own public recommendations on LinkedIn are the worked example", and llms.txt says "with his profile as the worked example". On /recommendations/ that section is a "Waiting for Dinis" placeholder, so the card promises something the page does not yet have. The CV card says "We describe his deck", but the page can only give its title and link. Fix: say what is there now, for example "Dinis's LinkedIn recommendations will be the worked example once he has answered our questions" and "We point you to his deck, sum up his argument, and give a 10-slide outline you can follow."

4. **Minor. /recommendations/ "The worked example", /cv/ slide 9, home card: the fact that he has public recommendations has no source.** The research notes record only a search snippet showing a "recommendations received" heading. The real source is Dinis's own brief (2026-11-19, about 14:30 UTC), which speaks of "his public recommendations". Fix: cite the brief for this, for example "(Dinis Cruz, brief to the team, 19 November 2026)", and do not imply the snippet.

5. **Minor. /cv/ "His argument" and "How he used it in hiring": several points are not in the research notes.** These are: content over polish (plain slide over buzzwords); asking for the deck later in the process and sometimes making it optional; candidates given guidance; the "learning plan and home lab" detail; "makes them better". I checked each one against the essay (https://diniscruz.ai/2025/06/08/using-presentations-instead-of-cvs-in-hiring.md, read 2026-11-19) and each is supported there, so nothing is invented. They are not traceable from team/research/2026-11-19-sources.md, though, and the sentence "He describes the process as one that "makes them better"" has no source marker. In the essay, those words come from his LinkedIn post as quoted there. Fix: add an essay source marker to the "makes them better" sentence ("in a LinkedIn post quoted in the essay") and add the points to the research notes.

6. **Minor. /cv/ "His argument": who wrote the essay.** The essay is written in the third person ("As security leader Dinis Cruz has argued"), and his about page says many pieces on his site are co-written with LLMs, which the bylines name. The page calls it "his essay" and summarises it as "his argument". That is fair, since it is published on his own site under his name. Even so, one clause such as "published on his site; he notes many of his pieces are co-written with LLMs" would be more exact. Optional.

Both are sourced, so this is not copying in bulk. Still, his own words should appear as his. Fix: put the slide 3 line in quote marks as his words, or reword it.
