# The power of recommendations

A CV says what you say you did. A recommendation says what someone else saw. This page explains why that matters, and how to ask for and write good ones. The advice here is from the AgentiCCells team unless it says it is Dinis's.

## Why everyone needs them

- **They are evidence from someone else.** Anyone can write a strong CV about themselves. A recommendation is a named person putting their own reputation behind what they say about you.
- **They describe what a CV cannot.** What you were like to work with, how you handled a hard week, what changed because you were there.
- **They last.** Jobs end and teams scatter. A recommendation written while the work is fresh stays on your profile after the people have moved on.
- **They are searchable.** On LinkedIn they sit on your public profile, where a recruiter or a future colleague can find them without asking you.
- **They are worth keeping.** Dinis's LinkedIn Vault proposal treats recommendations as career data, listing them with connections and accomplishments as records worth backing up to your own storage. ([LinkedIn Vault proposal](https://diniscruz.ai/2025/03/03/linkedin-vault__professional-data-preservation-servic.html)) Whatever tool you use, keep your own copy.

## What a good one says

A good recommendation is short and specific. It answers five questions:

1. **The situation.** What was the project, problem or team?
2. **What the person did.** Their actions, in plain words, not adjectives.
3. **The effect.** What changed because of it.
4. **The relationship and the time.** How the writer knows them, and for how long.
5. **Would you work with them again?** Say so, and in what role.

Three to six sentences is enough. One real example beats a list of qualities.

## How to ask for one

- **Ask the right people.** Pick people who saw your work closely: a manager, a peer, someone you managed, a client. Ask for a mix over time, not only managers.
- **Ask at the right time.** At the end of a project, when someone moves on, or soon after they thank you for something. Memory fades.
- **Make it easy.** Remind them of the project and the dates. Offer two or three points they might mention. Say how long it needs to be.
- **Give them an out.** Say clearly that it is fine to say no, or to say not now.
- **Say thank you,** whether or not they write it.
- **Offer one back,** if you can honestly write one for them.

### An example request

> **Example written by the AgentiCCells team. It is not a real message.**
>
> Hi [Name], I am updating my LinkedIn profile and would value a short recommendation from you, if you have time. You might mention [the project] in [year], when I [what you did]. Three or four sentences is plenty. If you would rather not, or are too busy, that is completely fine. I would be glad to write one for you too.

## How to write one for someone else

A structure that works:

1. **Open with the relationship.** How you know them and for how long.
2. **Give one specific example.** The situation and what they did.
3. **Say what changed.** The effect on the work, the team or the customer.
4. **Name a quality, with proof.** One thing they are good at, tied to the example.
5. **Close with your view.** Would you work with them again, and in what role.

### An example recommendation

> **Example written by the AgentiCCells team. It is not a real recommendation.**
>
> I managed [Name] for two years at [Company], where they led [project]. When [problem] put the launch at risk, [Name] [what they did], and the team shipped on time. They explain hard things simply, and people seek them out for it. I would hire [Name] again for any role that needs [skill].

### What to avoid

- Adjectives with no example: "great", "passionate", "a rock star".
- Writing about yourself more than about them.
- Anything they would not want a future employer to read, even meant kindly.
- Length for its own sake. A short, specific note reads as more honest.
- Agreeing to write one you cannot write honestly. Say no kindly instead.

## Checklist

- [ ] My CV or deck tells my story in my own words.
- [ ] I have at least three recommendations, from different kinds of working relationship.
- [ ] Each one names a situation, an action and an effect.
- [ ] I asked at the right time, made it easy, and gave people an out.
- [ ] I said thank you, and offered one back.
- [ ] I have written at least one for someone else this year.
- [ ] I keep my own copy of my recommendations.
